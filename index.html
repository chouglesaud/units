First, the idea

What is a unit?

A unit is an agreed amount we compare things to. "5" means nothing on its own; "5 metres" tells everyone exactly how long, because we all share one metre.

The whole world runs on one shared system called SI (from the French Système International). It has just 7 base units — things measured directly, like length, mass and time. Everything else is a derived unit: a recipe of base units. Speed is length ÷ time. A newton is kilogram·metre ÷ second². Pin down the seven, and the other two dozen fall out of them.

Below, the system is laid out in parts. Each measure gets a symbol, a plain definition, something you can play with, and a few everyday things that are "about one" of it.
